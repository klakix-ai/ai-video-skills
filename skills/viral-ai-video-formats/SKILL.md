# Viral AI video formats

Numbers below come from frame-by-frame analysis of viral AI reels (several with 0.7M+
views) and from our own account data. Small samples: treat them as strong hints, not laws.

## What the winners share

1. **One idea per video**, understood without sound or text.
2. **The hook is visual and early.** In the "history + modern object" pattern the absurd
   element appeared by 1.3 s at the latest, often in the first frame. No title cards, no
   talking head intro.
3. **Calm camera, real-looking footage.** The most-liked video in one set was 480p with the
   calmest camera. Resolution and fast camera did not drive success.
4. **Landscape for cinematic pieces.** Viral cinematic AI videos are mostly 4:3
   (1440x1080) or 16:9, 24 fps. Vertical is for talking-to-camera and tutorials.
5. **No speech, or very little.** Music-only pieces collected comments in five languages.
6. **A spoken call to action inside the video** beat a caption-only CTA by a wide margin
   (comment-to-like ratio 5.38% vs 0.26 to 0.54% in one comparison).

## Format 1: History + one modern object

A historical world where every shot uses **the same kind of modern object** for the same
job (canoe → jet ski, carriage → supercar, scroll → smartphone). Everything else stays
period-correct, and the locals treat the object as normal.

- 6 to 10 shots, one joke each.
- Characters dress for the era (generate costumes as references).
- Shot list per joke: setup (wide) → the object in use (medium) → a reaction (close).
- Avoid named historical figures, real brands, flags and political symbols.
- Variations that worked for us: medieval "rich flex" (ATM pouring gold, money gun,
  drifting carriages), ancient Egypt with a robot vacuum, a Trojan horse with a hidden
  vlogger.

## Format 2: Delayed reveal ("wait, is this real?")

The video proves its own claim. In the strongest examples the reveal came at **3.8 to
6.7 s**; weaker ones revealed at 0 to 2.2 s (4 of 4 videos, a strong hint).

- Open on something plausible, filmed like real footage.
- Build with **macro proof**: pores, iris detail, sweat, fabric. In the record-holding
  video macro shots were 23% of the runtime.
- Reveal with one beat, then hold.
- Edit: straight cuts only, no transitions, no speed ramps, no grain. Captions white,
  bold, 1 to 3 words, one accent colour.
- Audio: music absent or -8 to -10 dB under speech; music drops out ~0.6 s before the key
  line; the CTA plays without music; cuts do not follow the beat but the *event* lands on a
  musical drop.

## Format 3: Bystander phone POV

An impossible event filmed by an ordinary person: a dinosaur in a residential street from
a neighbour's balcony, a UFO at a concert from a fan's phone.

- Every shot is "the same neighbour's phone" or "a broadcast camera" — never cinema.
- Phone-zoom steps as the only camera moves (snap zoom + hold).
- Same street, same balcony across shots (edit the first key frame to make the rest).
- Ambient sound and a short, real-sounding reaction line beat music here.

## Format 4: Two-character scene

Selfie talking-head formats fatigue quickly: one account's selfie series fell from 674K to
32K views over repeats; their record was broken by a **new format**, a two-character
interrogation-room scene at 4:3 with a static camera and ~2.6 s median shot. Rotate
formats before they wear out.

## Calls to action that work

- Spoken by the character, in the video, max ~10 words: "Comment EGYPT and I'll send you
  how we made it."
- Comment-to-DM automation turns it into leads. Expect 94 to 96% of comments to be the
  trigger word: comment count then measures CTA response, not general interest.

## AI labelling

Platforms (Instagram since late August 2026) reduce distribution of AI-generated people
accounts and posts that are not labelled. Always:

- set the platform's "AI-generated" / "Made with AI" flag;
- say in the caption that nothing was filmed and which tools made it, including the music.

## When a good-looking AI video does not get reach

Signals from our account (37 reels, 30 days): views split into two clusters, 107 to 254
and 855 to 1,501, and the split was **not** explained by image quality. Videos with ad-like
signals (big branded hook text, a "Model X · Mode Y" credit line, a 3.5 s black logo end
card) stayed in the first test pool (n=3, a strong hint). What to try first, at zero cost:

1. Re-edit the same footage three ways: (A) raw "found phone footage", (B) delayed reveal,
   (C) with a narrator voice.
2. Remove ad signals: end card, model credits, captions that give away the reveal.
3. Post as trial reels, compare 48-hour numbers, put next week's budget into the winner.

## Concept checklist

- [ ] Understandable in the first frame without sound?
- [ ] One idea, one object, one world?
- [ ] Every shot has its own joke or proof?
- [ ] Hook ≤ 1.3 s (history/absurd) or reveal at 4 to 7 s (delayed reveal)?
- [ ] 30 to 40 s total, shots ≥ ~2.6 s, key moments 4 to 5 s?
- [ ] No real people, brands, flags; AI label set?
- [ ] Spoken CTA inside the video?
